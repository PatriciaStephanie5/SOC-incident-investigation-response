# GDPR Article 33 – Supervisory Authority Notification (Training Draft)

> Training artefact only, not legal advice or a completed regulatory filing. Validate facts and consult the organisation's DPO/legal counsel.

## What happened
Falkenstein Automotive GmbH became aware of a simulated personal-data breach involving a phishing-led endpoint and account compromise. A malicious attachment resulted in malware installation and credential compromise. The compromised account was then used to access information across several departments. A ransomware event followed.

## Data and people potentially affected
The scenario indicates access to information in HR, Procurement, Finance and Engineering. The employee's email address and login credentials, together with other people's data, were described as being in intruders' hands. Exact categories and numbers of affected people and records were not provided.

## Likely consequences
Potential consequences include account takeover, impersonation, targeted phishing, further unauthorised access, misuse of personal information and ransomware-related disruption.

## Measures taken
The endpoint was isolated and quarantined, firewall controls were applied to block attacker access, and the affected password was reset. Further recommended actions include revoking active sessions/tokens, reviewing access logs and investigating potentially affected accounts.

## Timing
GDPR Article 33 generally requires notification to the competent supervisory authority without undue delay and, where feasible, within 72 hours after awareness of a personal-data breach, unless it is unlikely to result in a risk to individuals' rights and freedoms. If notification is later, reasons for delay must be provided. Document the breach and assessment even where notification is not required.
